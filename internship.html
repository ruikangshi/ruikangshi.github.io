Overview

This internship placed me at one of China's most prestigious automotive AI labs, where I worked alongside PhD candidates and professors exploring the boundaries of autonomous vehicle safety. While the senior team tackled the core coding and simulation architecture, I took ownership and delved into the theoretical groundwork behind autonomous vehicle drift control. Through close interaction with the team and my independent study of drift control research, I developed a foundational understanding off how theoretical vehicle dynamics translate into practical autonomous driving, knowledge that I aim to apply as the team prepares for their second Hitch Open World AI Championships.

The team I joined was developing autonomous driving algorithms for two parallel tracks: passenger vehicle safety and autonomous racing. While the racing platform (used in the Hitch Open World AI Championships) served as a high-speed testbed, the ultimate goal was to translate aggressive maneuvering techniques—like drift—into emergency systems for consumer EVs.
